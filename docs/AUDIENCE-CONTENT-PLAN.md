# Government affairs and distressed-debt audience journeys

## Positioning

The shared opening is **“Power shifts. Exposure follows.”** It connects political research to two institutional mandates without promising foreknowledge, investment returns, or a capability the working plan does not establish.

The homepage pairs a fictional industrial visual with three concise claims. Government affairs and distressed-debt claims lead to dedicated explanations, fictional sample briefs, and a sector-preset enquiry. The methodology claim explains how a judgment should change with evidence. The form asks which decision matters and how a first briefing should demonstrate value.

## What to publish for each audience

### Oil & gas government affairs

1. A **decision-pathway brief**: the formal process, actors with documented authority, the next milestone, and unanswered implementation questions.
2. A **stakeholder position note**: stated positions and evidence of change, with dates and source links. Avoid inferring private intentions from attendance or association.
3. An **asset-context briefing**: local observations relevant to an operating footprint, compared with the official position and clearly marked where unresolved.

Package these for an internal briefing or an upcoming stakeholder conversation. Useful calls to action ask for the reader's policy milestone, asset footprint, and decision deadline.

### Distressed-debt investors

1. A **thesis-question register**: specific political or operational assumptions, observable outcomes, dates, evidence rules, and what would challenge the view.
2. A **catalyst note**: a named institutional or documentary development and the research questions it opens. Keep a possible catalyst distinct from a forecast of price movement.
3. A **counterparty evidence brief**: a source-linked comparison of statements, documents, and operational observations, with unresolved contradictions retained.
4. An **asset viability note**: documented criteria and evidence gaps, separated from security valuation, legal conclusions, and transaction recommendations.

Package these for an investment-committee research discussion. Useful calls to action ask which assumption in the thesis needs evidence and when the decision must be made. Do not ask for confidential positions or source-identifying material through the public form.

## Reuse the evidence; change the question

One approved source packet can support both audiences. A government affairs version asks who controls the process and what implementation means for an operating asset. A credit version asks which thesis assumptions need review. Do not change the underlying facts, increase confidence, or add a market conclusion to make a piece appeal to the second audience.

Before drafting, fill the common editorial brief and source register. Then write the audience-specific central question and intended meeting context. Show development status wherever a demonstration could be mistaken for a running product.
