## Part 1. What EchoFrame is

### The sentence

EchoFrame builds a continuous, dated record of what is happening in the parts of emerging markets that only produce snapshots, by paying local newsrooms for the reporting and the archives they already have, and checking what they report against official records, physical data and markets.

### The problem, said plainly

Most political and operational risk happens in places that do not produce a steady stream of information. A field in Monagas. A mining municipality in Bolívar. A border district in Pakistan. A supply route through eastern Colombia. Reporting from those places is thin, scattered across small outlets, and almost never confirmed twice.

So the industry works in snapshots. A company needs to know something, commissions a report, gets an answer as at that date, and waits until the next question. Every piece of work starts from nothing. Nobody is watching in between.

Snapshots are useful. They are also why most risk work arrives late. A pattern that only shows over months is invisible to a piece of work that looks at one week.

### What we do about it

Three things, in order.

**We pay for collection where there is none.** Local newsrooms in emerging markets have the two things this work needs and cannot monetise either: reporters in the right towns, and years of archive. We license the archive and pay for structured reporting alongside their normal work. The correspondents stay inside their own newsroom under their own editors. They get a revenue line that does not depend on advertising. We get collection no aggregator reaches.

**We check it against everything else that touches the same place.** Official records and gazettes, court and sanctions filings, company disclosures, satellite and physical signals, market data. Each is a different class of source. A development counts as confirmed only when independent classes agree, and news media count as one class however many outlets carry it, because three outlets in one city reporting the same thing is one source with three mastheads.

**We turn it into a record you can act on.** Every development has a date, a place, a type, the sources behind it and a status saying how well supported it is. From that record we answer a small number of written questions that have dates on them, and we score ourselves when those dates arrive.

### The method, in the Frame Bureau's own words

Six steps, a loop, not a pipeline. Collect, verify, structure, weigh, decide, check. The first three are the evidence layer, the last three are the judgement layer, and the loop closes because a person reviews the calls that missed and changes the criteria.

Inside it are five operations that take what a good editor or analyst does by instinct and write it down so it can run continuously and at scale.

1. Comparison against a baseline. Is this new or normal. You can only answer that if you are holding a model of normal. An editor has it in their head after twenty years on a patch. We have it in the archive.
2. Source weighting. Who is telling me this, and why now. The same sentence carries different weight from a ministry press office, a Maracaibo correspondent and an anonymous channel.
3. Significance. Does it change anyone's position, can it be reversed, how close does it sit to the client's exposure.
4. Implication. Given this, what becomes more likely. This is the step that separates knowing from judging.
5. Allocation. Cover or ignore. Alert or hold. Task a correspondent or wait. The output of judgement is a decision about scarce attention, not a summary.

This is already written for the Frame Bureau. It belongs on the public site too, because it is the clearest statement of what the company actually does.

### Where the EU tracker fits

It looks like a different business and it is not. In Venezuela the problem is that almost nothing is written down. In Brussels the problem is that everything is, continuously, in volumes no team can read. Both are the same failure: a public record that exists but cannot be used as a record. The same six steps handle both. One is scarcity, one is volume.

### Where this goes next

Venezuela is the working programme. Two years of archive, a structured event record for the eastern oil states, and a correspondent agreement in progress.

Next are Colombia, Mexico, Rwanda and Pakistan. Each chosen for the same three reasons: real capital exposure outside the capital, thin local reporting, and a newsroom or institutional relationship we can build the collection on.

### Two lines the company also sells

**The Frame Bureau.** The training division. It takes an institution's own people through the six steps until they can run a live desk, and leaves them the desk, the written procedures and the training process. Built for government and institutional clients who want the capability inside rather than rented.

**Capability build.** For a state or a large institution, the same thing at scale: designing and standing up an internal open-source intelligence function, with the work done jointly and handed over. Say it as capability transfer, not as a product.

### What to stop saying

Cut from the site: convergence, signals, frames, layers, pre-news window, decision-grade, anticipatory governance, and every two-line headline built on a slash. If a sentence would sit unchanged on a competitor's site, it is not telling anyone what we do. Also cut any claim that a method is in use when it is not. Prospect theory is not implemented. Five named scenarios have been replaced by dated questions.
