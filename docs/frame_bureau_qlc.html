The Frame Bureau

The Frame Bureau is EchoFrame's training division. It works with your trainees to bring them into EchoFrame's structured judgment model, which combines investigative journalism, data science and political risk analysis.

Structured judgment

Structured judgment is a six step process, shown in the loop below. It runs from collecting data, through structuring the data, to deciding the political implications of that data.

It is built as a loop because the method depends on people reinforcing it. Human judgment starts at collection, in deciding what we want to collect and why. That overlaps with AI tools that do the collecting and the first pass of processing at scale. We use investigative journalism to verify what arrives. We then structure it into a form a machine learning model can read, and we use political risk analysis together with the model to work out what is happening.

The five operations

Structured judgment takes the decisions a good editor or intelligence analyst makes by instinct and makes each one explicit, so they can run continuously, at scale, on live data, with people owning the criteria. It is also the support structure for origination, meaning the collection of information that does not yet exist anywhere else. Five operations, in sequence.

1. Comparison against a baseline
Is this new or normal. You can only answer that if you are holding a model of normal. An editor has it in their head after twenty years on a patch. We have it in the archive and in actor histories. A PDVSA statement is noise if they make one every month and a signal if it breaks the pattern. The judgment sits in the memory, not in the statement.
2. Source weighting
Who is telling me this, and why now. The same sentence carries different value from a ministry press office, a Maracaibo correspondent and an anonymous Telegram channel. Every input is multiplied by a credibility and motive coefficient before it is allowed to move anything. The words are identical. The judgment is the coefficient.
3. A significance function
A mapping from item to importance, built from stated criteria. Does it change anyone's position, can it be reversed, how close does it sit to the client's exposure, does it predict anything. That is what the signal scoring taxonomy is, an editor's instinct written down as weighted criteria. Domain experts own it, not engineers.
4. Inference of implication
Given this, what becomes more likely. This is the step that separates knowing from judging. The item is connected to a model of how the situation develops, and the probabilities of the outcomes move. An editor does it as this smells like escalation. We do it as a quantified shift.
5. An allocation decision
The output of judgment is never a summary. It is a decision about scarce resources. Cover or ignore. Alert or hold. Task a correspondent or wait. Pick up the phone or do not. This step spends the client's attention for them, and it is where intelligence becomes action.
Case study

Venezuela

In October last year, a US hedge fund holding distressed Venezuelan debt came to us with a bespoke question. They wanted the pressure points watched: unrest on the streets, the risk of the armed forces fracturing or moving against the government, the cohesion of the elite around Maduro, and the pressure the United States was applying to Caracas.

We ran it as a time series. Every day's reporting is turned into dated events with named actors, and those events move a set of readings we track separately rather than collapsing into one number. Through the second half of November we could see spikes in geopolitical pressure and in elite fracture, running for almost two weeks.

On 2 December we made the call. Forty per cent likelihood of a major political rupture within thirty days.

Every point computed forward only, using events first seen on or before that date.

The reading behind the call was the gap between the three lines. Geopolitical pressure was climbing and jumped again in the last week of November. Elite fracture had been falling all autumn and turned up off its trough at the start of December. Military went the other way entirely: it fell steadily from October and sat at its lowest point, 59.8, on the day we made the call. So the pressure from outside was building and the circle around Maduro was starting to come apart, while the armed forces were still holding.

Our judgement was that the military line could not hold against the other two, and that when it went it would go quickly. That is why the call was a rupture rather than a slow decline, and why we put it at forty per cent rather than higher. Military went from 60.2 on 19 December to 78.1 on 3 January.

Maduro was taken on 3 January, thirty two days after the call.

Interrogate the desk

Put questions to it yourself

This is the desk's own analyst, answering from the material above and nothing else. It has to cite what it used, it is not allowed to hedge, and if you ask it what would change its mind it will tell you. In a seminar the trainees try to break it, and their mark depends on whether they managed.

The programme

Five modules. Each one adds steps of the loop

The course is the loop, taken apart and put back together. Each module adds steps until the group is running all six on a live desk. There are no exams. Every module ends in something a real reader could use, and the last one is settled by what actually happened.

The bench

Taught by the people who do it

For the Qatar Leadership Centre

How it would start

We would design this with the Centre. The steps we propose are these.

1. Scoping
We work together to identify the desk the group would run, who the trainees are, and the problem that needs solving. Out of that we choose the country desk and the questions it should answer.
2. Design
Four to six weeks working with you and your faculty to write the pilot syllabus together, so the case material fits your trainees, the Centre’s priorities and the way we work.
3. Pilot group
We start with modules one to three, with clear markers, deliverables and measures for every trainee, so we can see that the process is working for them rather than assume it.
4. Full programme
All five modules. The written procedures and the desk itself are built over the course of it, because the whole thing is designed to be handed over.

What the Centre keeps

A programme where the trainees do the work and come out of it as editorial scientists, able to staff their own analytical desk within two to four weeks of finishing the five modules. The desk, the written procedures and the training process stay with the Centre and its faculty.
