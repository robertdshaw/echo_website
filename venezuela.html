Where the programme stands

The product framework brings risk dimensions, scenarios, and commercial criteria together. The asset-level examples below are fictional walkthroughs of the evidence process, not current findings or a live forecast feed. Collection scope, delivery cadence, and access are agreed for each engagement.

01 / The unit of intelligence

A question you can resolve

A country score cannot tell you whether a particular asset will keep operating. Define the outcome and the evidence needed to establish it.

Illustrative question / Q-DEMO-01FICTIONAL ASSET

Will Terminal A experience a continuous work stoppage of at least 48 hours between 1 and 30 June 2027?

Subject
Terminal A · hypothetical facility
Resolution date
1 July 2027
Resolution evidence
Two independent reports, or a field report plus an independently sourced media report, establishing the duration.
If evidence is insufficient
Mark the question unresolved. Absence of a report does not establish that no stoppage occurred.

A worked editorial example. No probability is assigned, and no real facility or incident is described.

03 / Proposed collection priorities

Three zones
Specific local questions

Initial collection priorities are Zulia, Monagas, Bolívar, and Anzoátegui. These are programme priorities, not a claim of contracted field coverage.

Oriente

Anzoátegui & Monagas

Proposed focus: the José complex, fields and upgraders, and the connections between operations, labour, contractors, and local authorities.

  • Loading, shut-ins, restarts, and inputs
  • Arrears, stoppages, and contractor payment
  • Access, community concerns, and local control
Occidente

Zulia; Falcón as scope expands

Proposed focus: the Lake Maracaibo area and, as demand supports it, the Paraguaná refining area.

  • Field and refinery operating conditions
  • Spills and fishing-community concerns
  • Contractor access and infrastructure
Guayana

Bolívar; Delta Amacuro as scope expands

Proposed focus: power, industrial activity, mining-related questions, and potential extension to Delta assets.

  • Grid reliability and industrial constraints
  • Local authority and community positions
  • Navigation and asset access

Collection scope depends on the questions commissioned, source access, and the ability to report safely. A more detailed map should only appear when the evidence supports that precision.

04 / Two views of the same asset

Operational evidence
Documented viability

A report about activity and a judgment about an asset’s contractual position answer different questions. The intended brief presents them alongside one another.

Six asset-specific checks

  • Contract standing
  • Licence and sanctions standing
  • Arbitration exposure
  • Counterparty solvency
  • Operational access
  • Security of tenure

These document checks sit beneath the wider six decision tests: rule of law, enforceable contracts, predictable rules, infrastructure, credible institutions, and policy continuity. A check without supporting evidence remains unassessed.

05 / Product direction

A brief you can trace
A forecast you can review

The question register

Named subjects, fixed wording, deadlines, resolution rules, and the assumptions behind each initial assessment.

The zone brief

A dated event record, the accounts that support or dispute it, location precision, and an analyst’s next verification step.

The resolution record

Forecast revisions preserved before outcomes are known. Results assessed against a defined evaluation policy and benchmark.

These examples explain the brief structure. Forecast performance needs a complete dated record, defined resolution rules, and a benchmark; the selected historical case above is not that evaluation.
